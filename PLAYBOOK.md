# First-Client Playbook: AI Receptionist / Chatbot via Facebook DMs

Goal: 1–2 paying clients this month. Read Section 1 first.

---

## 1. Brutal honesty (read this before doing anything)

**What I could not do, and what I am not sure about**
- I cannot log into Facebook, browse Marketplace, or send DMs for you. I don't have your account. Every DM below is a template you personalise by hand, using real details from each lead's page.
- I have not verified live Meta policy or current per-account message limits. Meta changes them without notice and doesn't publish exact numbers. Treat the limits below as conservative rules of thumb, not guarantees. No one can promise "no flag."
- The numbers below are **my estimates from general cold-outreach experience, not measured data.** Your results will vary a lot.

**Realistic funnel for a beginner, cold DMs, small-business owners**

| Stage | Optimistic | Realistic beginner |
|---|---|---|
| DMs sent | 100 | 100 |
| Seen (not buried in "Message Requests") | 40–60% | 20–40% |
| Replies | 8–12 | 3–8 |
| Positive replies (willing to talk) | 3–5 | 1–3 |
| Calls booked and held | 2–3 | 1–2 |
| Clients closed | 1 | 0–1 |

- **25 DMs will most likely produce 0 clients.** Expect about 0–2 replies. A sample that small is mostly luck.
- To get 1–2 clients in a month, plan on **150–250 quality touches** (DMs + follow-ups + a few calls/walk-ins/emails), not 25. At about 12 DMs a day, 30 days gets you about 300 touches, so it is possible. It is not easy.
- **Probability of ≥1 paying client within 30 days:** roughly 40–60% if you follow this plan with discipline. About 15–25% if you send 25 DMs and stop. 1–2 clients is a stretch goal. Plan for 1.
- Beginners' main failure modes: (1) giving up after 20–30 ignored DMs, (2) pitching "AI chatbots" instead of a business result, (3) targeting people who can't or won't pay, (4) not following up, (5) building a demo nobody asked for.

**Facebook Marketplace is the wrong place to find clients.**
Marketplace is a classified-ads system for goods. Most sellers are individuals selling a couch. Even the business sellers (used cars, appliances, furniture) are a poor fit: they list on Marketplace to sell items, and a buyer message is already their lead flow. A few Marketplace listers *are* real businesses (car dealers, mattress stores, detailers, movers), so you can use it to *spot* businesses, but don't rely on it.

**Better lead sources on Facebook (same effort, better fit):**
1. **Google Maps + the business's Facebook Page.** Search "[niche] in [your city]", find ones with a Facebook Page, real reviews, and *signs of missed leads* (see Section 4).
2. **Facebook Page "Ads Library"** (facebook.com/ads/library, public). A business running ads pays for leads and cares about lead response. This is your best filter: they are already spending money.
3. **Local FB groups** (business owners, "[city] small business", niche groups). Contribute helpfully first. Do not spam-pitch, because it gets you removed and reported.
4. **Marketplace**, only to spot real businesses (detailers, movers, salons, repair shops).

**Other honest points**
- Personal-profile DMs to strangers land in "Message Requests" / "Spam" folders. Many owners never see them. Messaging from a **Facebook Page** you own to a business Page is often seen more, because the business's Page inbox is monitored. Do both.
- A parallel channel will beat DMs alone: **walk-ins, calls, and email** to local businesses convert better than Facebook DMs for many niches. If you want a 1–2 client month, don't rely on a single channel. Plan for roughly 60% DMs and 40% other.
- Your first client will probably pay **little** ($200–$500 setup, maybe $50–$150/month). That is normal. The goal is a case study and a testimonial. If you hold out for $2,000 deals in month one, you'll probably close nothing.
- "AI" is not what sells. **Missed calls and slow replies losing them money** is what sells.
- You will get rejected or ignored about 90% of the time. That's the base rate, not a sign you're bad.
- Don't buy courses, follower packs, or "guaranteed client" services. Most are a waste.

## 2. Polish your Facebook profile (do this in one evening, 1–2 hrs)

You have a 10-year-old account. That's an asset (older accounts get more trust from Meta). Don't do anything that looks like a sudden change in behaviour (see Section 6).

**Profile checklist**
- [ ] **Profile photo:** clear, friendly, well-lit headshot, plain background. No group shots, no filters, no logos. 
- [ ] **Cover photo:** simple banner, e.g. "I help [niche] never miss a customer call or message." Made in Canva (free).
- [ ] **Name:** your real name. Don't put keywords in your name field.
- [ ] **Intro/bio (under 100 chars):** `I set up AI receptionists so [niche] stop losing leads to missed calls.`
- [ ] **Work/"Featured" section:** "Founder at [Your Brand]" (make a simple name; a Page is optional).
- [ ] **Location:** your real city/region. People trust local.
- [ ] **Contact:** one professional email. Add a Calendly/Cal.com link only *after* you have a conversation, never in the first DM.
- [ ] **Featured section:** pin 1 post: a 30-second demo video (screen recording of the bot/receptionist answering a realistic customer question for a fake local business).
- [ ] **Posts:** publish 3–5 posts over 1 week *before* messaging anyone. Example topics: "Why businesses lose 30–40% of inbound calls after hours" (only state a number you can source, otherwise say "many"), a demo clip, a short before/after of a chatbot answering FAQs.
- [ ] **Privacy:** make the profile publicly viewable (Friends-only looks hidden and untrustworthy to a stranger checking you out). Remove anything embarrassing or political.
- [ ] **Turn on two-factor authentication.** A hacked or locked account = game over.
- [ ] **Create a Facebook Page** for your service (2 mins). Use it for posting and for messaging business Pages.
- [ ] **Add real friends who are business owners** (the people you already know). Ask 5–10 people you know for a review/recommendation.

**Do NOT:** buy fake likes/followers, mass-add strangers as friends, join 30 groups in one day, change name/photo repeatedly.

## 3. Niche selection (pick ONE, commit for 30 days)

Selection criteria: (a) inbound calls or messages = revenue, (b) each lead is worth a lot, (c) the owner answers the phone personally and often can't, (d) active on Facebook, (e) small enough that the owner decides alone, (f) you can explain the value in one sentence.

| Niche | Lead value | Owner decides alone? | Missed-call pain | My rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Home services (plumbers, HVAC, electricians, roofers)** | High ($300–$10k jobs) | Yes | Very high (on the job, can't answer) | **Best pick** |
| Med spas / aesthetic clinics | High | Usually | High | Good, but pricier clients and harder to reach |
| Dental / chiro clinics | High | Sometimes (office manager) | High | Good, but gatekeepers |
| Real estate agents | High | Yes | High, but heavily pitched already | Saturated |
| Salons / barbers | Low–medium | Yes | Medium | Low budgets |
| Auto detailers / car repair | Medium | Yes | High | Decent fallback |
| Restaurants | Low | Yes | Medium | Low margin, low budgets, avoid |
| Gyms/fitness studios | Medium | Yes | Medium | Fine fallback |
| Lawyers | Very high | Yes | High | Compliance/confidentiality risk, later |

**Recommendation: local home-service businesses (plumbing, HVAC, electrical, cleaning, pest control, roofing) in your own city/region.** Fallback: auto detailing/repair.

Why: a single missed call is a $200–$2,000+ job, so paying you $300 + $100/month is an easy ROI to explain. You can do the math in the DM.

**What you sell (one offer, not a menu):**
> **"Missed-call text-back + 24/7 AI receptionist."** When you miss a call or a Facebook message, the AI instantly replies, answers common questions (hours, pricing range, service area), collects name/job/address, and books or flags the job to your phone.

Deliverables: (1) missed-call text-back, (2) Messenger/website FAQ + lead-capture bot, (3) optional AI voice receptionist, (4) lead alerts to the owner by SMS/email.

**Pricing for a beginner (suggested):**
- **Pilot offer for first 2 clients:** setup $250–$500 one-off + $99–$199/month, cancel anytime after 30 days. Or 14-day free trial only for the first client, in exchange for a written testimonial.
- Do not do "free work" for a dozen prospects. One free pilot, in exchange for a testimonial and a referral.
- Raise prices to $500–$1,500 setup and $200–$500/month after 2–3 clients.

## 4. Finding and qualifying leads (the part that decides your success)

Work from a spreadsheet (Section 8, `lead-tracker.csv` in this repo).

**Qualify each lead before messaging. Only DM if ≥3 of these are true:**
- [ ] Business Page active in the last 30 days
- [ ] Phone number on the page, and reviews mention "didn't answer" / "hard to reach" / "slow to reply"
- [ ] Running Facebook/Google ads (check Ads Library)
- [ ] Small team (1–15 people), owner-run
- [ ] "Message" button visible but Page shows a slow response time ("Typically replies within a day")
- [ ] Closed evenings/weekends but customers likely need help then (plumbing emergencies, etc.)
- [ ] 20+ reviews (established, has money)

**Test them yourself (this is the best personalisation):**
- Call them once after hours, or message the Page at 9 pm. Note the exact response time or that nobody answered. Now you have a real, specific hook. (Don't be deceptive: it's fine to ask a genuine question about a service.)

**Skip:** businesses with no ads, no reviews, dormant pages, franchises/chains, and anyone in a regulated field you can't serve compliantly.

Target: build a list of **60 qualified leads in week 1**, message about 12/day.

## 5. The 20 DMs (templates you personalise)

I can't see your leads, so these are structured templates. **Every DM needs one real, specific detail per lead** (`[SPECIFIC]`): a review quote, a service they offer, their response time, a recent post. If you can't fill in a specific detail, don't send it. That's what keeps you out of "spam" territory and what gets replies.

Rules for all DMs: short (under ~60 words), no links, no attachments, no prices, no "AI" jargon, one soft question, no fake urgency, no claims you can't prove, and an easy out.

**Group A: Missed calls and slow replies (home services)**

1. `Hi [Name], I found [Business] while looking for [service] in [city]. Your reviews are great, especially the one about [SPECIFIC]. I messaged your page at 8 pm and haven't heard back yet, which makes me wonder if you lose jobs after hours. I help local [trade] businesses answer instantly when they're on a job. Worth a quick chat, or is that already handled?`
2. `Hi [Name], quick one. I tried calling [Business] on a Saturday and it went to voicemail. Guessing you were on a job! For [trade] businesses that's often the difference between winning a $[amount] job or not. I set up a simple text-back so missed calls get an instant reply. Would that be useful, or do you have this covered?`
3. `Hey [Name], I saw your Page says you usually reply "within a few hours". For emergency [service], people usually message 3 companies and go with whoever answers first. I build a simple auto-reply that qualifies the job and gets it to your phone. Open to hearing how it works?`
4. `Hi [Name], I noticed a review says "[SPECIFIC quote about hard to reach]". Not a knock on you, it's a common problem when you're running jobs yourself. I help owners fix it without hiring anyone. Would you like me to show you what it looks like for [Business]?`
5. `Hi [Name], big fan of the work in your [photo/post]. Do you have anyone answering calls when you're on-site, or does it go to voicemail? Asking because I'm setting up a few local [trade] businesses with an AI receptionist and want to learn how you handle it now.`

**Group B: They run ads**

6. `Hi [Name], I saw you're running Facebook ads for [service]. Do the leads from those ads get an instant response, even at night? Most businesses I look at lose a lot of ad spend to slow follow-up. I help fix that. Happy to explain in 2 minutes if useful.`
7. `Hi [Name], your ad for [SPECIFIC offer] caught my eye. Quick question: what happens when someone messages the ad at 10 pm? If the answer is "they wait until morning", I have a simple fix. Mind if I share how?`
8. `Hi [Name], you're spending on ads to get [service] leads, but if the Page answers slowly, the money's wasted. I set up instant replies that qualify leads and book them in. Want a quick look at how it'd work for [Business]?`

**Group C: Local and community angle**

9. `Hi [Name], I'm a [city] local starting an AI receptionist service for small businesses here, and I'm looking for 2 owners to try it at a heavy discount in exchange for honest feedback. [Business] came to mind because [SPECIFIC]. Interested?`
10. `Hi [Name], fellow [city] person here. I'm building something for local trades that answers calls and messages when you can't. Not selling today, just looking for feedback from owners who know the pain. Could I ask 2 quick questions?`
11. `Hi [Name], I've seen you in [group name]. Great advice on [SPECIFIC]. I'm working on a tool that handles after-hours calls for local businesses. Would you be up for a 10-min chat about how you handle that now?`

**Group D: Curiosity and question-led**

12. `Hi [Name], quick question: how many calls do you think [Business] misses in a week? Most owners guess 2–3 and it's usually more. Curious what your number is.`
13. `Hi [Name], if a customer messaged your Page at 11 pm asking for a quote, what would happen?`
14. `Hi [Name], do you ever lose a job because you couldn't pick up in time? I help businesses like [Business] with that. Any interest in a quick chat?`

**Group E: Value-first / demo**

15. `Hi [Name], I made a 30-second demo of an AI assistant answering questions for a [trade] business like yours, using [Business]-style questions (hours, service area, "do you do emergencies?"). Want me to send it? No pitch attached.`
16. `Hi [Name], I put together a quick mock-up of how [Business]'s Facebook messages could be answered automatically 24/7. Happy to send it if you'd like a look. If not, no worries at all.`

**Group F: Referral / soft**

17. `Hi [Name], I don't know if you're the right person, but who handles calls and messages for [Business]? I have something that might save them time.`
18. `Hi [Name], I help small [trade] businesses stop losing jobs to missed calls. If that's not a problem for you, tell me and I won't bother you again. If it is, I'd love to show you how it works.`

**Group G: Trial offer**

19. `Hi [Name], I'm offering one [city] [trade] business a free 14-day trial of an AI receptionist (it answers missed calls and messages, and texts you the job details). I only need honest feedback after. Interested? I'm only taking one.` *(Only say "only taking one" if it's true.)*
20. `Hi [Name], I set up missed-call text-back for local businesses. Trial's free for 2 weeks. If it doesn't bring you at least one extra enquiry, you owe nothing. Want me to set it up for [Business]?`

**Follow-up sequence (this is where most replies come from)**
- Day 0: initial DM
- Day 3: `Hi [Name], just bumping this in case it got buried. Even a "not interested" helps me. Thanks!`
- Day 7: one value message: e.g. the demo clip, or one useful tip for their business (no ask).
- Then stop. No more than 3 messages per lead, ever.

**If they reply "what is it?" / "how much?"**
`Short version: when you miss a call or message, it replies to the customer within seconds, answers common questions, collects their details and texts you the job. Setup takes about a week. For the first couple of local businesses I'm doing it for $[X] setup and $[Y]/month, cancel any time. Easiest way to explain is a 15-min call. Are you free [day] or [day]?`

**If they say no / stop / not interested:** reply once with `No problem, thanks for letting me know!` and never message again.

## 6. Staying inside Meta's rules (and reducing ban risk)

Based on Meta's general Messenger/Community Standards (spam, fake engagement, automation). Re-read them yourself: Facebook Community Standards → "Spam", and Messenger Platform Policy. **I haven't verified the latest text, so treat this as a guide.**

**Do**
- Send every message **manually** from your account. No bots or scripts, no auto-DM tools on your personal profile. That is the fastest way to get restricted.
- Personalise each message and send to real businesses with a reason to hear from you.
- Keep to about **10–15 new DMs/day**, spread through the day, not in bursts. Ramp up: days 1–3 send 5/day.
- Stop when someone says no or ignores 3 messages.
- Be honest about who you are and what you're offering.
- Use **business Pages/Messenger for business enquiries** where possible.
- Use the Page "Message" button on business pages (it's what it's for).

**Don't**
- Copy-paste the identical message to many people (Meta detects near-duplicates). Vary wording each time.
- Include links in first messages (raises spam-detection risk).
- Send DMs to people who haven't got any relation to your offer.
- Use multiple accounts, fake profiles, or scrape data with tools.
- Promise guaranteed results or make false claims.
- Spam groups. Read the group rules and DM group members only if the group rules permit it.
- Send friend requests to 100 strangers.

**Warning signs to stop for 48+ hours:** "You can't send messages right now" banners, CAPTCHA/identity checks, sudden lower reach. If you get a restriction, stop DMing, don't create a new account, and follow the appeal flow.

**Honest risk:** even doing everything right, cold DMs from a personal profile carry non-zero risk of a temporary message block. Keep your account backed up: export your info, keep contacts' details in your spreadsheet/email, not just in Messenger, so you can move to email/phone if you get blocked.

## 7. The 30-day plan

**Week 0 (2 days): Setup**
- Profile + Page polish (Section 2)
- Pick niche + city; write the one-sentence offer
- Build the demo (see tools below): a missed-call text-back and a Messenger/website FAQ bot for a *fake* local plumber. Record a 30-sec screen video.
- Build your lead sheet.

**Week 1: Leads + warm-up**
- Build 60 qualified leads
- Post 3–5 useful posts, join 3–5 relevant local groups, comment helpfully
- Send 5/day, then 10/day
- **Also** phone/walk in/email 5–10 businesses (higher conversion than DMs for many people)

**Week 2: Volume + calls**
- 12 DMs/day + follow-ups, plus 3–5 non-Facebook touches/day
- Book calls. Prepare a call script (below)

**Week 3: Close**
- Run calls, offer the pilot, follow up hard on everyone who showed interest
- Onboard your first client the same week they say yes (speed = trust)

**Week 4: Deliver + get proof**
- Deliver, measure (calls answered, leads captured), ask for a testimonial and 2 referrals
- Keep prospecting. Don't stop when you get client 1

**Target metrics (weekly)**: 60+ new touches, 5+ replies, 2+ calls, 1 proposal. If after ~100 DMs you have <3 replies, change the message or the niche. That's the signal, not a reason to quit.

**Call script (15 min)**
1. Rapport (1 min): "Thanks for taking the time."
2. Questions (5 min): "How do calls/messages get handled when you're on a job? How many jobs do you think slip through in a week? What's an average job worth?" Do the math out loud: 3 missed jobs × $400 = $1,200/week.
3. Show (4 min): demo the bot/receptionist for their business.
4. Offer (3 min): pilot price, what's included, cancel-anytime.
5. Close (2 min): "Want me to get this live for you by [date]?" Then stop talking.

**Common objections**
- "Too expensive": "It pays for itself if it catches one extra job a month. Want to start with a 2-week trial?"
- "I don't trust AI with customers": "Fair. It only answers a fixed list of questions you approve, and anything else goes straight to you."
- "I'll think about it": "Sure. What would you need to see to decide? I'll follow up Thursday."

## 10. Bottom line

- 25 DMs will very likely not be enough. Aim for 150–250 touches across DMs, follow-ups and a few non-Facebook channels.
- Marketplace isn't where the buyers are. Use Google Maps, Ads Library and local groups, then reach them via their Page.
- Sell one thing: **never miss a customer call or message again**, to local home-service businesses, at a small pilot price.
- Your first client is about proof and a testimonial, not profit.
- Realistic outcome this month: 1 client, maybe 0, rarely 2. Keep going past the month if you land nothing. Month 2 is when the compounding starts.
